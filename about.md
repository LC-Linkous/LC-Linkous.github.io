I am an electrical engineer and research scientist in the College of
Engineering at Virginia Commonwealth University, working in computational
electromagnetics, machine-learning-assisted optimization, and embedded device
security. My Ph.D. in engineering focused on machine learning and optimization
for antenna design; the dissertation, on machine learning assisted optimization
for the calculation and automated tuning of antennas, is still the thread
through most of what I do: getting a design loop to run end to end without a
person driving every step of it. Before that: an M.S. in computer science and
B.S. degrees in electrical engineering and in physics, all at VCU.

Alongside the research is a second line of work in instrument software: browser
front ends and Python libraries for the RF test equipment I use, each with a
simulated device built in so that the tools can be used and taught with whether
or not the hardware is on the desk.

Teaching ties both of these together. I run the medical device security testbed
in the college, where student researchers learn hardware interfacing, firmware
extraction, and reverse engineering on real equipment, and much of the public
material here is the open half of units built for courses. Several of the lab
tools exist because a course needed them.

## Research

- Computational electromagnetics and antenna design
- Machine learning assisted optimization: swarm, derivative-free, and
  surrogate-assisted methods
- Machine learning and large language models for electromagnetics and RF design
- Software-defined radio and digital signal processing
- RF, medical device, and embedded device security; firmware extraction and
  reverse engineering
- Engineering education

Papers, posters, and news coverage are on the [Publications](/publications)

## Teaching

**Courses.** Fundamentals of electrical and computer engineering (an
introductory lab course where students build and measure working analog and
digital circuits, and a second course on static electric and magnetic fields),
and microwave engineering (a graduate course on RF components, antenna arrays,
and measurement with network, signal, and spectrum analyzers). Earlier, as a
teaching assistant and co-instructor: medical devices, assistive technology,
electric circuits, and introduction to engineering.

**Vertically Integrated Projects.** Multi-semester undergraduate research teams
that I design the curricula for and mentor: medical device security (hardware
reverse engineering, firmware extraction, wireless communication), machine
learning and mathematical optimization for RF design, large language models for
electromagnetics, and, earlier, the smart city testbed. These teams are where
the cryptography, reverse engineering, computer vision, and machine learning
units under [Projects](/projects/#teaching-material) come from.

**Senior capstone.** Faculty mentor for design teams including a modular
telehealth cart for disaster relief, a low-field portable MRI, and successive
Little Ram Pantries projects.

The browser-based lab instruments and wireless demos built for these courses
are collected at
[interactive-hardware-interfacing](https://interactive-hardware-interfacing.github.io/).
